Our Shared Project

We are always iterating on programming and community support, and awardees are how we learn what works. We read all of your emails and try to be as responsive as possible. It helps us do our jobs if you:

  • Keep your record current. Updated titles, affiliations and links let us make accurate introductions and programs.
  • Tell us what helps. Answer our surveys and check-ins, and say which opportunities are useful to you and which are not.
  • Share what you see and hear. How is your work funded, evaluated and rewarded? What gets in the way? Where is the leverage and where are things changing fast?
  • Try things with us. Be open minded about pilots, convenings, projects and new formats.

How we use what we learn. We use it in two ways. The first is to improve the support we offer you. The second is to understand the systems you work inside, such as newsrooms, universities and independent media, and where there is real leverage to change them. Conversations with journalist awardees, for example, have shaped how we think about what leverage exists in journalism.

SciComms Initiatives

The awards sit inside a wider set of efforts aimed at the places science communication is made and supported.

Science Friday

Science Friday is a public radio program and podcast that covers science and technology, from the outer reaches of space to the tiniest microbes in our bodies, in stories that are both entertaining and educational. Learn more.

Transom Story Lab

Transom helps people tell powerful true stories in sound, and has facilitated storytelling training at the 2025 and 2026 annual workshops. We are funding Transom to train more story coaches who can go out and help scientists create the best stories they can. The train-the-trainers program started in 2026. Learn more.

CASW and the State of Science Journalism

The Council for the Advancement of Science Writing works to improve the quantity and quality of science news reaching the public. We are developing a multi-year State of Science Journalism report with CASW. Learn more.

Frequently Asked Questions

About the Program

The Eric and Wendy Schmidt Awards for Excellence in Science Communications, given by the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine in partnership with Schmidt Sciences, recognize excellence in science communication across platforms and media. We hope to inspire a new generation of journalists, researchers and creators to engage the public with complex ideas in STEM, to encourage continuing excellence in the field, and to help awardees become even more effective communicators.

The program runs in two phases. In the first, the National Academies run an open call, and a jury and selection committee choose 24 awardees each year across three categories (Science Journalist, Research Scientist and Science Communicator), with subcategories by career stage and setting. In the second, Schmidt Sciences supports what follows: an annual workshop and ceremony, training and convenings with partners, Catalyst Grants, and openings that put awardees in rooms with scientists and funders. Many of these opportunities come up during the year, so expect invitations beyond what is listed here.

Who do I ask about prize money, payment and taxes?

The National Academies program staff listed in the Contact section. They can answer questions about prize payments and the tax treatment of the award.

How should I refer to the award?

Use the full name on first mention: the Eric and Wendy Schmidt Awards for Excellence in Science Communications, presented by the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine with support from Schmidt Sciences. Preferred shorter forms: Schmidt SciComms Awards.

Can I use the National Academies or Schmidt Sciences names and logos?

You may state your award accurately in bios, CVs and websites. For logos, or for any use that suggests an endorsement, ask first. For National Academies requests, email Olivia Hamilton at ohamilton@nas.edu. For Schmidt Sciences requests, use the Communications request form.

Who handles press inquiries about the award?

Send them to the National Academies and Schmidt Sciences so the two organizations respond consistently. For the National Academies, email Olivia Hamilton at ohamilton@nas.edu. For Schmidt Sciences, use the Communications request form.

What should I do if I have a high-profile mention of the award or of Schmidt Sciences?

Please let the Schmidt Sciences Communications team know. You can email comms@schmidtsciences.org or use the communications submission form.

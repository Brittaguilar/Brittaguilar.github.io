What this training is meant to do

Lower the barrier

A short virtual session reaches more people than an in-person workshop can seat, and gives everyone a shared vocabulary for how good stories are built.

Learn before January

It shows The Open Notebook what SciComms Awardees want ahead of the in-person workshop, and tests whether this kind of training is useful beyond the SciComms Awardee community, with Schmidt Science Fellows as a pilot group.

Why The Open Notebook

Who they are

The Open Notebook (TON) is a nonprofit science journalism organization founded in 2010 by freelance science journalists Siri Carpenter and Jeanne Erdmann. Its mission is to help science journalists improve their craft.

  • Publishes interviews with journalists on how specific stories were built, plus articles on reporting, pitching and ethics
  • Runs a database of pitch letters and a directory of freelance science writers
  • Led by Siri Carpenter (Executive Director and Editor-in-Chief) and Rachel Zamzow, PhD (Deputy Executive Director, who leads training)
Why we want to engage them
  • Training is their specialty. They teach how science stories are reported, structured and told, the skill SciComms Awardees asked for.
  • Ready-made case studies. Their 2026 book, The Best Science Stories and How They Work, annotates published stories the session can build on.
  • One partner across both trainings. TON also leads the January workshop, so the two can build on each other.

Before

A one-page announcement shares the date, time, suggested pre-work, and what to expect, so people can hold the date. The Open Notebook runs a platform check ahead of the event.

During

Participants receive a copy of the book. Schmidt Science Fellows join as a pilot group, and also act as a focus group on how useful this is for scientists.

After

A short survey asks about usefulness and intent to apply the techniques. The Open Notebook writes a debrief report by the end of December.

After the virtual session

A deeper workshop in January, and a plan to learn from both.

The virtual session is the on-ramp. The Open Notebook designed the fall topics to relate to narrative writing without overlapping the workshop, so someone can attend both.

Narrative Writing Workshop, January 24–27, 2027

What it is

A three-day, in-person intensive on writing clear, accurate and engaging narratives. It builds on the fall session with hands-on instruction and practice.

  • Held at the Banbury Center, Cold Spring Harbor Laboratory
  • About 20 participants (the venue seats up to 30)
  • Likely restricted to SciComms Awardees. Schmidt Science Fellows are not part of this workshop.
  • Banbury handles applications, selection and logistics
How the days run
  • Sun, Jan 24: opening dinner
  • Mon–Tue, Jan 25–26: core instruction
  • Wed, Jan 27: morning instruction and closing lunch

Instructors from The Open Notebook are Siri Carpenter, Rachel Zamzow and Kate Fishman, joined by two additional instructors. SciComms Awardees are under consideration for those two roles.

We are trying to measure the impact and effectiveness of this kind of training on scientists, not only whether people enjoyed it. This session is one piece of a wider effort to build evidence on science communication, which includes other impact measurement projects across the portfolio.
